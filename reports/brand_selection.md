# Brand Selection: AmazonHelp

## Selected Brand

**AmazonHelp**

## Selection Rationale

AmazonHelp was selected because it provides a large, diverse, and conversation-rich customer-support corpus suitable for all three components of the proposed agent:

1. Intent classification
2. Historical-resolution retrieval and grounded reply drafting
3. Risk-aware escalation

## Dataset Evidence

Using the `thoughtvector/customer-support-on-twitter` dataset, we independently profiled the AmazonHelp account and reconstructed reply-thread structure.

### Conversation Definition

A conversation is defined as a connected reply thread containing at least one direct customer -> AmazonHelp interaction.

Conversation roots were reconstructed by following `in_response_to_tweet_id` links backwards from tweets participating in AmazonHelp customer interactions.

This produces **82,534 unique conversation roots**.

## Why AmazonHelp?

The corpus provides:

- substantial support volume
- a large number of customer -> brand interactions
- substantial multi-turn interaction
- diverse operational support workflows
- enough historical responses to support retrieval-based generation
- enough difficult cases to study escalation decisions

## Alternatives Considered

Other large support brands, including Tesco and VerizonSupport, were considered.

The final selection was not based on raw tweet volume alone. We considered:

- conversation volume
- conversational depth
- recurring support patterns
- historical customer -> brand resolutions
- issue diversity
- potential for meaningful escalation decisions

## Why Not Simply Choose the Largest Dataset?

Raw volume can be misleading. A large corpus may contain many repeated examples of a small number of high-frequency issues, which can make both training and evaluation appear stronger without providing broader operational coverage.

Therefore, brand selection considered both **quantity and quality of conversational evidence**.
